# Industry Research: Benchmarks, Trends and Best Practices

This document summarizes the research that informed the T&D model design. Each item is linked to a design decision for GASM.

## 1. Context: mining and steel in Mexico

| Trend | Description | Implication for T&D |
|---|---|---|
| **Nearshoring** | Automotive, appliance and electrical-equipment investment in Nuevo León, Coahuila and the Bajío raises demand for automotive-grade steel and for technical talent. | Strong competition for PLC, hydraulics and electrical technicians. GASM must *build* talent internally (apprenticeships, academies) rather than only *buy* it. |
| **Tariff and cost pressure** | US Section 232 steel tariffs and the flow of imported steel into Mexico squeeze margins. | Training must prove its business impact: productivity, yield and reliability. |
| **Crisis of a large integrated producer (Coahuila, 2023–)** | Experienced steelworkers entered the labor market. | A short-term chance to hire experienced workers, who then need *onboarding plus certification*, not full training. |
| **Mining safety scrutiny** | High-profile accidents (such as the 2022 El Pinabete coal mine flooding in Sabinas, Coahuila) increased STPS inspection activity and social pressure. | Rigorous, auditable competence verification for critical risks. |
| **Mining Law reform (2023)** | Shorter concession terms and stronger water, environmental and community obligations. | Training on community relations, environmental and water stewardship. |
| **Decarbonization** | DRI + EAF is already a low-emission route. Hydrogen-ready DRI, electrification and scrap quality are the next steps. | New "green steel" skills: hydrogen safety, energy management (ISO 50001), advanced scrap sorting. |
| **Automation and digitalization** | Autonomous drilling, fleet management systems, digital twins, predictive maintenance, remote operating centers. | Digital skills for operators and maintainers; data literacy for supervisors. |
| **Aging workforce** | Many experienced workers in the sector are close to retirement. | Structured knowledge capture, mentoring and succession planning. |
| **Women in mining and industry** | The sector has one of the lowest female participation rates in Mexico. CAMIMEX and companies have inclusion initiatives. | Inclusive programs, targeted apprenticeships, and adapted facilities and PPE. |

## 2. Benchmark: training investment and intensity

The values below are *reference ranges* from public industry studies (for example ATD *State of the Industry* reports and sector sustainability reports). Update them with current data before a board presentation.

| Indicator | Heavy-industry reference range | GASM today | GASM target (year 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training hours per employee per year | 40 – 70 h | 38 h (≈ 70% compliance courses) | **56 h** (≤ 40% compliance, ≥ 60% performance) |
| Spend per employee | USD 700 – 1,300 | ≈ USD 500 (MXN 9,200) | ≈ USD 890 (MXN 16,400) |
| Spend as % of payroll | 1.5 – 3.0% | ≈ 1.8% | ≈ 3.0% |
| T&D staff ratio (including full-time instructors) | 1 : 100 – 1 : 200 | 1 : 310 | **1 : 133** |
| Share of training delivered by internal instructors | 50 – 70% | 39% | **65%** |
| Share of critical roles with a certified successor | 60 – 80% | < 20% | **75%** |

## 3. Best practices adopted by the GASM model

### 3.1 Learning models

| Practice | Source or rationale | Where it appears in the GASM design |
|---|---|---|
| **ADDIE / SAM** instructional design | Established instructional design methods | Design & development process (TD-P04) |
| **70-20-10** (experience, social learning, formal learning) | Leadership development research | OJT, mentoring and job rotation paths in all academies |
| **Competency-based training and assessment (CBTA)** | Mining-sector practice (Australia, Chile, Canada) | Operator certification scheme (TD-P07) |
| **Critical Control Management** | ICMM (2015) | "Critical Risk Standards" program with field verification |
| **Simulation-based training** | Haul-truck, shovel and crane simulators show gains in fuel efficiency, component life and safety | Mining and steel academies |
| **VR/AR for high-risk tasks** | Safe practice of LOTO, confined space and molten-metal emergencies | VR lab at each Academy campus |
| **Microlearning and mobile learning** | Shift work and remote locations | LMS mobile app; 5–10 minute pre-shift modules |
| **Knowledge capture: *Expert Legacy*** | Aging workforce | Structured interviews, video SOPs, mentor–apprentice pairs (TD-P10) |
| **Dual training (Mexican Dual Training Model, MMFD)** | CONALEP, Universidades Tecnológicas | GASM Technical Apprenticeship program |

### 3.2 Evaluation

| Practice | Description |
|---|---|
| **Kirkpatrick levels 1–4** | Reaction, learning, behavior and results |
| **Phillips ROI (level 5)** | Monetizes the level-4 results, isolates the effect of training and computes ROI. Applied to 3–5 flagship programs per year only. |
| **Learning analytics** | LMS and HRIS data linked to safety, maintenance (CMMS) and production (MES) KPIs |

### 3.3 Governance

| Practice | Description |
|---|---|
| **Corporate University / Academy model** | A central Center of Excellence (CoE) sets standards. Site teams deliver. Business leaders sponsor each academy. |
| **Learning Council** | A quarterly executive forum that prioritizes investment against the business strategy |
| **Joint governance with the union** | CMCAP at each site, plus a corporate union–management training forum. This is a legal requirement and it builds legitimacy. |

## 4. Lessons learned from the sector (what fails)

1. **"Attendance = competence."** Many serious accidents involve workers who had "taken the course" but had never been assessed on the job. → GASM certifies competence in the field and re-certifies it.
2. **Training centers without operators' time.** Expensive centers sit empty because production does not release people. → Protected training time is built into the roster and tracked as a KPI owned by line managers.
3. **Contractors outside the system.** → Contractors go through the same critical-risk certification. Competence clauses go into REPSE contracts.
4. **Programs imported without adaptation.** Content in English or at the wrong reading level does not transfer. → All operational content is in Spanish, visual, and validated with operators.
5. **Big-bang LMS rollouts.** → Pilot at one site, then scale. Offline and kiosk access for the mines.
6. **No link to *escalafón*.** The union resists programs that do not lead to promotion. → Training paths are aligned with the CCT and co-designed in the CMCAP.

## 5. Potential partners (Mexico)

| Type | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Technical education | CONALEP, Universidades Tecnológicas (UT), Tecnológico Nacional de México (TecNM) campuses near the sites | Dual apprenticeships, instructor exchange, shared labs |
| Universities | Regional public universities (UANL, UAdeC, UdeC, UdeG) and private universities | Engineering internships, graduate programs, leadership programs, applied research |
| Industry associations | CAMIMEX (mining), CANACERO (steel), AIMMGM (mining engineers' association) | Sector competency standards, benchmarking, mine rescue competitions |
| Government | STPS, CONOCER, state employment services, IMSS (safety programs) | Compliance, certification, subsidies and support programs |
| OEMs and technology vendors | Mining equipment, EAF, automation and simulation OEMs | Equipment-specific certification, *train-the-trainer* |
